# References

All sources below were rechecked during this migration. Adoption means the stated concept is informative, not that an algorithm or claimed result is already implemented.

1. J. van der Meer, M. van der Woude, and W. M. P. van der Aalst, “Literature Survey on Automatic Pipe Routing,” *SN Operations Research Forum*, 4, 2023. DOI: [10.1007/s43069-023-00208-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43069-023-00208-5). Used for the separation of space, route, objective and constraint modelling, plus branching/resource-competition/multi-resolution concerns; not copied as a ready-made MEP solver.
2. G. Belov, W. Du, M. Garcia de la Banda, D. Harabor, S. Koenig, and X. Wei, “From Multi-Agent Pathfinding to 3D Pipe Routing,” *SOCS*, 11(1), 11–19, 2020. DOI: [10.1609/socs.v11i1.18530](https://doi.org/10.1609/socs.v11i1.18530). Used for the MAPF-to-spatial-pipe-routing analogy and conflict-aware planning; moving-agent time semantics are not copied directly.
3. G. Sharon, R. Stern, A. Felner, and N. R. Sturtevant, “Conflict-based search for optimal multi-agent pathfinding,” *Artificial Intelligence*, 219, 40–66, 2015. DOI: [10.1016/j.artint.2014.11.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artint.2014.11.006). Used as the CBS reference: high-level constraints with single-agent constrained replans; not selected as the only production coordinator.
4. H. Ma, D. Harabor, P. J. Stuckey, J. Li, and S. Koenig, “Searching with Consistent Prioritization for Multi-Agent Path Finding,” *AAAI*, 33(1), 7643–7650, 2019. DOI: [10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33017643](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33017643). Used for partial priority ordering and conflict-driven branching; its theoretical guarantees do not automatically transfer to MEP constraints.
5. J. Li, Z. Chen, D. Harabor, P. J. Stuckey, and S. Koenig, “Anytime Multi-Agent Path Finding via Large Neighborhood Search,” *IJCAI*, 2021, 4127–4135. DOI: [10.24963/ijcai.2021/568](https://doi.org/10.24963/ijcai.2021/568). Used for destroy/repair neighborhood recovery; it does not establish MEP engineering feasibility.
6. F. Zhang, K. Zhu, and A. Joneja, “Automated multi-pipe routing in MEP plant rooms: Preference-driven multi-objective coordination framework for design prototyping,” *Automation in Construction*, 2026, 107201. DOI: [10.1016/j.autcon.2026.107201](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2026.107201). Relevant concepts: coarse-to-fine planning, dynamic header connection points, conflict-aware arbitration and maintenance trade-offs; its plant-room scope differs from this general architecture.
7. IHNF, [MEP Plant Room Pipe Routing Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/IHNF/mep-plant-room-pipe-routing), CC BY 4.0, accessed 2026-10-01. The dataset self-identifies as case-level companion data to reference 6 and includes inputs, selected layouts, costs and optional Revit visualization. Use only after pinning a revision and confirming its license/provenance in the benchmark manifest.
8. buildingSMART, [IFC schema specifications](https://technical.buildingsmart.org/standards/ifc/ifc-schema-specifications/) and [IfcDistributionPort](https://technical.buildingsmart.org/projects/2024tunnel/schema/ifcsharedbldgserviceelements/lexical/ifcdistributionport.htm). Used for port, flow direction/system type and schema terminology; implementation must target a declared IFC release.
9. buildingSMART IFC 4.3 documentation, [IfcRelVoidsElement](https://ifc43-docs.standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4x3/HTML/lexical/IfcRelVoidsElement.htm), [IfcOpeningElement](https://ifc43-docs.standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4x3/HTML/lexical/IfcOpeningElement.htm), and [IfcRelConnectsPorts](https://ifc43-docs.standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4x3/HTML/lexical/IfcRelConnectsPorts.htm). Used for future opening/connection relationships; no IFC output is implemented.
